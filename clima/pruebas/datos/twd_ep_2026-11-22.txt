Tropical Weather Discussion for the eastern Pacific Ocean from
03.4S to 30N, east of 120W including the Gulf of California, and

...SPECIAL FEATURES...

Significant NW Swell Event: A new cold front will approach the
open high seas waters near 30N140W early Mon, moving southeast of
30N140W Mon night. Associated NW swell will arrive just out ahead
of it with rough seas pushing southeast of 30N140W by late Mon
morning. Seas will continue spreading southeast across the 
northern waters through the early part of the week, exceeding 12 
ft southeast of 30N140W Mon night. Seas of 12 ft or greater will 
cover the waters west of a line from 30N133W to 26N140W by late 
Tue morning, then from west of a line from 30N122W to 16N140W by 
late Wed morning. Seas of 12 ft or greater will continue 
spreading southeast beyond that while slowly decaying. Seas will 
peak at 19-22 ft near 30N140W.

...INTERTROPICAL CONVERGENCE ZONE/MONSOON TROUGH...

The monsoon trough extends from the Pacific coast of Colombia
near 05N77.5W to 01S83.5W to 02N93W. The ITCZ extends from 02N93W
to 06.5N120.5W, then resumes west of a surface trough from
05.5N124.5W to 05N140W. Scattered moderate to strong convection
prevails within 90 nm either side of the monsoon trough, from
03.4S to 00N between 116W and 120W, from 10N to 13N between 129W
and 135.5W, and from 00N to 10N between 135.5W and 140W.

...OFFSHORE WATERS WITHIN 250 NM OF MEXICO...

Surface ridging prevails across the area, with ASCAT-B and
ASCAT-C scatterometer data indicating light to gentle
anticyclonic winds offshore southern and southwest Mexico, and
gentle to moderate mainly northern winds elsewhere. Seas are 4-6
ft offshore southern Mexico, and 5-8 ft elsewhere, except 1-3 ft
in the Gulf of California. NW swell mainly contributes to these
seas, except SW swell dominating over the waters offshore
southern Mexico.

For the forecast, a small plume of fresh to strong northerly gap
winds will develop in the Gulf of Tehuantepec tonight, then pulse
through Tue night. A cold front will move through the Gulf of
America by mid-week, increasing and expanding these winds. Winds
may reach gale-force in the Gulf of Tehuantepec late Tue
night/early Wed morning, then persist to gale-force through Thu
night. Fresh to near gale-force winds will then persist through
at least the end of the week. Rough seas will accompany these
winds, reaching 12 ft or greater by late Wed morning. Moderate or
weaker winds will increase to moderate to fresh across the waters
from 17N to 27N tonight due to a tightening pressure gradient,
reaching strong speeds near Cabo Corrientes Mon afternoon and
evening. The moderate to fresh winds will expand northward and
encompass the Gulf of California and Baja California offshore
waters by early Tue, then confined to from the central Gulf and
Cabo San Lazaro northward by early Thu while pulsing to strong
speeds. These winds should diminish by the end of the week.
Meanwhile, old NW swell with seas to rough near the Revillagigedo
Islands will decay tonight. Looking ahead, a new set of rough to 
very rough NW swell will arrive offshore Baja California Norte 
Wed, spreading southeast through the end of the week.

...OFFSHORE WATERS WITHIN 250 NM OF CENTRAL AMERICA, COLOMBIA, 
AND WITHIN 750 NM OF ECUADOR...

Fresh northeast to east gap winds are ongoing in the Gulf of 
Papagayo per recent ASCAT scatterometer data. Moderate northerly 
winds are in the Gulf of Panama. Light to gentle winds prevail
across the remainder of the waters. Moderate seas in mixed old,
long-period SW and NW swell prevail, except for locally rough 
seas southwest of the Galapagos Islands.

For the forecast, fresh northeast to east gap winds will pulse each
night in the Gulf of Papagayo, reaching strong speeds at times,
and near gale-force by the end of the week as the pressure
gradient tightens. Seas will build to rough there by the end of
the week. Winds will pulse to moderate to fresh in the Gulf of 
Panama at night through much of the week. Light to gentle winds 
will prevail elsewhere. Moderate to rough seas from offshore 
Ecuador to the Galapagos Islands will persist through the early 
part of the week before subsiding. Looking ahead, rough seas from
an upcoming Gulf of Tehuantepec gap wind event may impact the 
offshore waters of Guatemala by mid-week, with moderate seas 
elsewhere.

...REMAINDER OF THE AREA...

Please refer to the Special Features section for details on an
upcoming Significant NW Swell event over the northern waters.

A surface trough is analyzed from 16N118W to 05N123W. Associated
scattered moderate to strong convection is found from 15N to 19N
between 109W and 115W. The pressure gradient between the trough 
and ridging west-northwest of it supports moderate to fresh 
trades from 08N to 24N between 120W and 140W. Resultant wind 
waves are mixing with old long-period NW swell to support 
combined seas of 7 to 10 ft from 05N to 23N between 104W and 
140W. A cold front extends over the far northern waters from 
30N126W to 27N135W. Associated NW swell supports seas of 7-9 ft 
behind the front. Across the southern waters, merging long-period
SW and NW swells supports combined seas of 7 to 10 ft across the
waters south of 05N between 94W and 122W. Moderate or weaker 
winds and moderate seas prevail across the remainder of the open 
waters.

For the forecast, the cold front in the northern waters will
dissipate through tonight, while the associated seas subside into
early Mon. Another cold front will move southeast of 30N140W by
Mon evening, with fresh to strong SW winds ahead of it over the 
northwest waters, and a new set of NW swell with rough seas. 
Those seas will spread southeastward with rough to very rough 
seas across the northern waters as described above through much
of the week. Meanwhile, moderate to fresh trades will persist 
across the central waters through the early part of the week,
increasing to fresh to strong by mid-week as the gradient behind
the front tightens. Associated seas will be moderate to rough. 
Merging SW and NW swells over the south-central waters will 
linger through the early part of the week before subsiding.
Looking ahead, another significant NW swell event may build seas
back to 12 ft or greater over the far northwest waters by the end
of the week into next weekend.
